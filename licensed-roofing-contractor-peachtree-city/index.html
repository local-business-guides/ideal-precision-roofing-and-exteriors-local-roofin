What to check in the scope and estimate

A roof assessment is the first step toward deciding whether repair or replacement makes sense. The estimate should let you see what the provider observed and how the proposed work addresses it. Ask how the timing is expected to work; inspection, material selection, the size of the job, and conditions during the project can all affect the schedule. There is no single timeline that fits every roof.

Key facts to review:
- Coverage: Residential and commercial inspections, repairs, and replacements.
- Materials and systems: Asphalt shingles, metal roofing, and commercial TPO and EPDM systems.
- Cost factors: Roof size and condition, chosen materials, and the amount and type of work affect the overall estimate.
- Timing: Ask for a project-specific schedule and what could change it.

When you compare providers, put proposals side by side. Check that each describes the same areas of the roof, materials, repair or replacement work, and cleanup. Ask what is excluded and how any additional work would be explained. A clear answer makes it easier to compare the actual work, not just the total figures.

A few terms you may hear: roof inspection means an assessment of roof condition; scope is the written description of planned work; TPO and EPDM are types of commercial roofing membrane systems.
